# State-of-the-art pairwise entity matchers (PLM and LLM) and how they compare with feature-based GBDT matchers (2021-2026)

Scope and vocabulary used throughout these notes:
- **Pairwise matcher**: a model that takes two records (A, B) and outputs P(match). Blocking/candidate generation is out of scope here.
- **PLM matcher**: a fine-tuned encoder language model (BERT / RoBERTa / DeBERTa / DistilBERT, 66M-350M parameters) that reads the two serialized records jointly ("cross-encoder") and outputs a match probability. Ditto, HierGAT, R-SupCon, Unicorn, AnyMatch belong here.
- **LLM matcher**: a generative model (GPT-4, Llama, Qwen, Mistral...) prompted with the pair and asked "same entity? yes/no", either zero-shot, with a few examples in the prompt (few-shot / in-context), or after fine-tuning.
- **Feature-based matcher**: hand-crafted string-similarity features (Jaccard, Levenshtein, TF-IDF cosine...) fed to a classical classifier. **Magellan** (Konda et al., PVLDB 2016) is the standard academic example: automatically generated per-attribute similarity features + scikit-learn Random Forest. This is the closest academic analogue of our LightGBM-over-~70-features system.
- **Benchmarks**: the "ER-Magellan" / DeepMatcher datasets (Abt-Buy, Amazon-Google, Walmart-Amazon, DBLP-ACM, DBLP-Scholar(=DBLP-Google), iTunes-Amazon, Beer, Fodors-Zagats, Company), split 3:1:1 train/validation/test; "Dirty" variants move attribute values into the wrong columns at random. WDC Products (Peeters, Der, Bizer, EDBT 2024) is the harder product benchmark with controlled "corner cases" (look-alike non-matches) and "unseen" entities. All F1 numbers below are F1 of the "match" class on the fixed test split, in percent, unless stated otherwise. None of these papers reports F0.5, which is what we optimise.

## Q1. Pre-trained LM matchers (Ditto, HierGAT, JointBERT-style, Sudowoodo, R-SupCon, Unicorn, AnyMatch): F1, backbones, label needs, inference cost

### Takeaway
With in-domain labelled training pairs (thousands), a fine-tuned 110M-350M cross-encoder (Ditto/HierGAT/RoBERTa) reaches ~99 F1 on DBLP-ACM, ~95-96 on DBLP-Scholar, ~86-88 on Walmart-Amazon, ~89-94 on Abt-Buy and ~75-79 on Amazon-Google; the successive "SOTA" PLM variants (HierGAT, DAEM, AttendEM, R-SupCon) differ from Ditto by only ~0.2-3.5 F1 on average. The big gains of PLMs over Magellan-style feature models are on dirty/textual data (e.g. Dirty Walmart-Amazon 85.7 vs 37.4); on clean structured data the gap is small, and on harder benchmarks with look-alike non-matches or unseen entities every PLM loses 10-25+ F1 points.

### Cited Findings
**Ditto (Li, Li, Suhara, Doan, Tan; PVLDB 14(1), 2021)**
- Casts EM as sequence-pair classification over a pre-trained LM; RoBERTa-base was the default backbone for ER-Magellan (BERT for Company, DistilBERT for WDC); adds domain-knowledge tagging, long-string summarisation and data augmentation. Reported F1 = average of 5 runs. — [Ditto PVLDB paper](https://www.vldb.org/pvldb/vol14/p50-li.pdf)
- Ditto test F1 (vs best prior deep model "DM+"): Structured Amazon-Google 75.58 (DM+ 70.7), Beer 94.37 (78.8), DBLP-ACM 98.99 (98.45), DBLP-Google 95.6 (94.7), Fodors-Zagats 100 (100), iTunes-Amazon 97.06 (91.2), Walmart-Amazon 86.76 (73.6); Dirty DBLP-ACM 99.03, DBLP-Google 95.75, iTunes-Amazon 95.65, Walmart-Amazon 85.69 (DM+ 53.8); Textual Abt-Buy 89.33 (62.8), Company 93.85 (92.7). Training-set sizes (all pairs, 3:1:1 split): Amazon-Google 11,460; Beer 450; DBLP-ACM 12,363; DBLP-Google 28,707; iTunes-Amazon 539; Walmart-Amazon 10,242; Abt-Buy 9,575; Company 112,632. — [Ditto PVLDB paper](https://www.vldb.org/pvldb/vol14/p50-li.pdf)
- Plain fine-tuned RoBERTa without Ditto's optimisations ("Baseline" column) already gets most of the gain: e.g. Walmart-Amazon 85.81, Abt-Buy 88.85, Amazon-Google 74.10, DBLP-ACM 98.96. The paper states the LM accounts for 78.5% of Ditto's average improvement over DM+ on ER-Magellan. Company (Wikipedia articles about companies, 3,123 words average) scored only 41.00 without summarisation (sequence length 256) and 93.85 with it. — [Ditto PVLDB paper](https://www.vldb.org/pvldb/vol14/p50-li.pdf)
- Label efficiency: "In 4/5 cases, when trained on less than 20% of the original training data, Ditto is able to achieve close or even better performance than DM+ when the full training sets are in use"; on WDC (215k training pairs) Ditto 94.08 F1 with all data, 84.36 with 1/20 of the data. — [Ditto PVLDB paper](https://www.vldb.org/pvldb/vol14/p50-li.pdf)
- Real-world company matching: on two company datasets of 789K and 412K records Ditto "achieves a high F1 score of 96.5%". Running-time table for that pipeline: Sentence-BERT encoding 2,229 s on GPU, similarity search 1,982 s on CPU, Ditto matching of the top-10 candidates per record 1,339 s vs 22,823 s for matching all basic-blocking candidates (hardware: AWS p3.8xlarge, V100 GPUs). — [Ditto PVLDB paper](https://www.vldb.org/pvldb/vol14/p50-li.pdf)
- Reproducibility caveat: an independent re-run of Ditto without its external-knowledge and two optimisation components got results "consistently" lower than the paper, "on average by 25%" (e.g. Walmart-Amazon 58.02 at 40 epochs vs 86.76 reported), while other re-implemented transformer matchers (EMTransformer-RoBERTa: Walmart-Amazon 85.04, Abt-Buy 87.11-89.29) reproduced close to published numbers. — [Papadakis et al., arXiv 2307.01231 (2023)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.01231)
- Licence: Ditto code Apache-2.0. — [megagonlabs/ditto on GitHub](https://github.com/megagonlabs/ditto)

**HierGAT (Yao et al., SIGMOD 2022), DAEM (Huang et al., 2022), AttendEM (Low, Fung, Xiong; Knowledge-Based Systems 293, June 2024)**
- Published F1: HierGAT Amazon-Google 76.4, Beer 93.3, DBLP-ACM 99.1, DBLP-Scholar 96.3, iTunes-Amazon 96.3, Walmart-Amazon 88.2, Dirty Walmart-Amazon 86.3, Abt-Buy 89.8, Company 88.2. DAEM: Amazon-Google 73.1, DBLP-Scholar 97.3, iTunes-Amazon 100, Walmart-Amazon 87.7. **R-SupCon (Peeters & Bizer, arXiv Feb 2022)**
- Supervised contrastive pre-training of a RoBERTa encoder, then a fine-tuned classification head. Reported Abt-Buy 94.29 F1 (+3.24 over previous SOTA) and Amazon-Google 79.28 (+3.7); WDC Computers +0.8 to +8.84 depending on training size. — [Peeters & Bizer, arXiv 2202.02098](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.02098)
- On WDC Products (medium dev set, 50% corner cases) R-SupCon is best on seen products (81.88) but drops to 57.23 on unseen products ("a large drop of 25%"); authors conclude "the contrastive pre-training thus severely limits the generalization ability of this approach". — [WDC Products paper, EDBT 2024 (arXiv 2301.09521)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.09521)
- Licence: code BSD-3-Clause. — [wbsg-uni-mannheim/contrastive-product-matching](https://github.com/wbsg-uni-mannheim/contrastive-product-matching)

**WDC Products benchmark results (Peeters, Der, Bizer; EDBT 2024)** — training sizes: small = 2,500 pairs (500 pos), medium = 6,000 (1,500 pos), large ~19-20k (~8k pos); test = 4,500 pairs (500 pos).
- F1 at 80% corner cases, seen / half-seen / unseen test sets: Large dev set — Word co-occurrence+LinearSVM 56.67/50.24/30.26; Magellan (similarity features + Random Forest) 31.96/36.42/34.95; RoBERTa-base 78.15/75.52/69.75; Ditto 79.46/68.81/67.94; HierGAT 75.42/73.20/68.53; R-SupCon 82.15/67.27/53.31. Small dev set — Magellan 31.15/33.75/33.34, RoBERTa 65.45/66.68/64.50, Ditto 58.33/58.97/57.16, R-SupCon 77.48/64.25/51.91. At 20% corner cases, Large: Magellan 37.58/41.57/37.23, RoBERTa 87.80/82.17/78.64, Ditto 87.52/82.81/77.92, R-SupCon 89.04/74.59/62.45. — [WDC Products paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.09521)
- "Increasing the amount of corner-cases causes all methods to lose performance ... negatively impacts the precision of matchers more than it does recall", i.e. the matchers mistake "very similar negatives as positives". Unseen entities also mainly hurt precision. — [WDC Products paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.09521)
- Training setup used: RoBERTa-base, 50 epochs, batch 64, lr 5e-5, early stopping; R-SupCon contrastive stage 200 epochs, batch 1024. — [WDC Products paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.09521)

**Sudowoodo (Megagon, ICDE 2023) — self-/semi-supervised contrastive**
- Reported to reach "up to 16%" F1 gains over SOTA in low-label settings "with one-third fewer labeled training instances" — [Sudowoodo arXiv 2207.04122](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.04122). In ComEM's comparison on 8 datasets its mean F1 is 70.32 vs Ditto 80.69 and HierGAT 83.34 (supervised). — [ComEM, COLING 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.16884v3)
- Licence: BSD-3-Clause. — [megagonlabs/sudowoodo](https://github.com/megagonlabs/sudowoodo)

**Cross-dataset (zero target labels) PLM matchers: Unicorn and AnyMatch** — setting: train on the other 10 benchmark datasets, test on the held-out one ("leave-one-dataset-out").
- Mean F1 over 11 unseen target datasets: StringSim 43.5; ZeroER 57.1; Ditto (110M) 72.9; Unicorn (143M, DeBERTa-base, SIGMOD 2024) 81.0; AnyMatch-GPT-2 (124M) 81.5; AnyMatch-T5 (220M) 78.6; AnyMatch-Llama3.2 (1.3B) 87.5; Jellyfish-13B 84.7 (contaminated on 6 datasets); MatchGPT-GPT-4 87.4; MatchGPT-GPT-4o-mini 83.9. Per-dataset for AnyMatch-Llama3.2: Abt-Buy 89.3, WDC 69.4, DBLP-ACM 96.5, DBLP-Scholar 89.8, Amazon-Google 69.3, Walmart-Amazon 77.2. — [Zhang, Groth, Calixto, Schelter, "A Deep Dive Into Cross-Dataset Entity Matching with Large and Small Language Models", EDBT 2025](https://openproceedings.org/2025/conf/edbt/paper-224.pdf)
- "Fine-tuned small models can perform on par with prompted large models" and "can be deployed at an orders of magnitude lower cost"; fine-tuned SLMs have higher run-to-run variance than LLMs; GPT-4o-mini beats the ~81-F1 small models by >10 points on WDC and Walmart-Amazon (domain-specific, ungrammatical product text). — [EDBT 2025 cross-dataset study](https://openproceedings.org/2025/conf/edbt/paper-224.pdf)
- AnyMatch (GPT-2, 124M, ~260 MB GPU RAM for weights): throughput ~694k tokens/s vs 26.7k for Jellyfish-13B and 2.1k for Mixtral-8x7B; estimated cost $0.0000038 per 1k tokens vs $0.015 for GPT-4 (3,899x cheaper); mean zero-shot F1 81.96 vs MatchGPT-GPT-4 86.36. — [AnyMatch, arXiv 2409.04073](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.04073)
- Licences: GPT-2 weights MIT ([HF](https://huggingface.co/openai-community/gpt2)); Llama-3.2-1B under the Llama 3.2 community licence, not Apache/MIT ([HF](https://huggingface.co/meta-llama/Llama-3.2-1B)); DeBERTa-v3-base/large and RoBERTa-base are MIT ([HF deberta-v3-base](https://huggingface.co/microsoft/deberta-v3-base), [HF roberta-base](https://huggingface.co/FacebookAI/roberta-base)).

**Architecture study (2026)**
- Controlled factorial study (Qwen3 family at three sizes, bi-encoder vs cross-encoder vs generative matchers, 9 datasets, 1,215 fine-tuning runs): cross-encoders keep "consistent advantages over bi-encoders"; generative matchers are not universally better, their gains "concentrate under distribution shift"; larger models "rely more heavily on shortcut learning". — [Zhang, Li, Calixto, Groth, Schelter, "Beyond Scale and Generation", arXiv 2607.24688 (July 2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24688)

### Inferences
- For our setting (plenty of labelled in-domain pairs, fixed schema of name/address/country), the relevant PLM configuration is a plain fine-tuned cross-encoder (RoBERTa/DeBERTa-v3/XLM-R, all MIT). Published evidence says Ditto's extra tricks add little on top of a plain fine-tuned LM, and newer PLM architectures add <1-3 F1 on average.
- A cross-encoder of this size is cheap enough to score only the pairs that survive blocking (the Ditto company case study scored top-10 candidates per record for ~789K records in ~22 minutes on a V100). That supports using it on candidate pairs only, not on all pairs.
- R-SupCon-style contrastive pre-training is risky if the test set holds entities never seen in training (France in our test set). WDC Products shows a 25-point drop on unseen entities.

### Gaps
- None of the papers report F0.5 or precision at high-recall operating points, so "gain in F0.5" cannot be read off directly; WDC Products Table 4 gives precision/recall only for neural systems.
- No benchmark in this literature is a business-registry dataset with transliterated Indian names; the closest are the "Company" dataset (Wikipedia text pairs) and Ditto's unpublished 789K x 412K company case study (data not released).
- Licences for Unicorn (ruc-datalab/Unicorn), AnyMatch (Jantory/anymatch) and ComEM code could not be confirmed: no licence label was detected on their GitHub pages on 2026-09-25, so treat them as "no licence" until checked by hand.
- Sudowoodo's exact per-dataset numbers were not extracted from the primary paper (a search snippet claimed Abt-Buy 58.82 vs Ditto 77.34 in some low-label setting; not verified, so not used).

## Q2. LLM matchers (GPT-4/4o zero/few-shot, fine-tuned open LLMs, Jellyfish, MatchGPT, batch/list-wise prompting) vs PLM matchers; which are Apache/MIT and <=8B

### Takeaway
Zero-shot GPT-4 roughly matches or beats fine-tuned PLMs on product benchmarks where it has not seen the training data (WDC Products 89.6 vs Ditto 84.9) but is worse on the bibliographic sets (DBLP-Scholar 89.8 vs 94.3). Open <=8B models are clearly weaker zero-shot (Llama-3.1-8B 53.4 F1 on WDC Products) and need fine-tuning or smarter prompting (select/compare strategies) to reach ~80 mean F1. The <=8B Apache-2.0 options with published EM numbers are Qwen2/2.5/3-7-8B, Mistral-7B and Flan-T5 (Apache-2.0). Jellyfish (CC-BY-NC-4.0) and Llama 3.x (Meta community licence) are not allowed under our licence rule.

### Inferences
- An open <=8B LLM used zero-shot per pair is not competitive with a fine-tuned cross-encoder (Llama-3.1-8B 47.75 mean F1 in matching mode vs Ditto 80.69 on the same ComEM datasets). A 7-8B model is only useful here if fine-tuned (LoRA) or used in compare/select mode, and even then reaches ~80-81 mean F1, about the same as a fine-tuned 125M-350M encoder that costs 20-100x less to run.
- The benefit that LLMs show consistently is robustness to distribution shift (unseen datasets/entities). For us that matters mostly for the France slice of the test set. A fine-tuned Apache-2.0 Qwen 7-8B scoring only the uncertain band of LightGBM scores is the defensible LLM use; scoring every candidate pair is not.
- Batch/list-wise prompting (BatchER, ComEM "select") maps naturally to our "one query record vs its blocked candidates" structure, but position bias is a known failure mode, so candidate order would need randomising or several orderings averaged.

### Gaps
- No published EM result for Qwen2.5/Qwen3-7-8B fine-tuned on a company-name benchmark was found; ComEM's Qwen2-7B numbers are prompting-only.
- Throughput numbers for fine-tuned 7-8B matchers on commodity GPUs were not found in the sources read (the EDBT 2025 study reports 26.7k tokens/s for Jellyfish-13B on its hardware).
- Contamination: the authors note it is "impossible to determine" whether GPT models saw the public benchmarks, so zero-shot GPT-4 numbers may be optimistic.

## Q3. Feature-based (Magellan / GBDT) matchers vs learned text matchers, evidence on hybrids/stacking, and character-level models (ByT5, CANINE)

### Takeaway
On clean, structured, short-attribute data, similarity-feature models come close to deep models: Magellan-RF gets DBLP-ACM 98.3 vs 99.0 and Fodors-Zagats 100, and 6 of 13 standard datasets are over 0.8 "linearly separable". They collapse on dirty or long-text data (Dirty Walmart-Amazon 37.4 vs 85.7; Abt-Buy 43.6-51.3 vs 89.3) and on WDC Products corner cases (Magellan ~31-38 vs RoBERTa 65-88). We found no peer-reviewed benchmark study that stacks a PLM score into a GBDT over similarity features; the only direct hybrid evidence is one non-peer-reviewed industrial preprint (+4.6 F1 from adding Levenshtein-based fuzzy scores to a transformer). ByT5/CANINE are Apache-2.0 and documented as more robust to spelling noise and better at transliteration, but no EM benchmark result for them was found.

- Re-run Magellan variants (same blocked candidates as the DL methods): Magellan-RF DBLP-ACM 98.32, DBLP-Scholar 92.96, iTunes 89.66, Walmart-Amazon 67.76, Beer 84.85, Amazon-Google 56.10, Abt-Buy 51.30, Company 100.00; Magellan-LR Company 100.00. Magellan averages: Structured 88.8, Textual 83.4, Dirty 68.5 vs DeepMatcher 87.9 / 88.0 / 87.9. — [Papadakis et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.01231); [AttendEM preprint summarising Mudgal et al. 2018](https://dmas.lab.mcgill.ca/fung/pub/LFX24kbs_preprint.pdf)
- "Most of the popular datasets pose rather easy classification tasks"; "the linearity of six datasets exceeds 0.8 (with three exceeding 0.9)"; among structured datasets only Walmart-Amazon and Amazon-Google show both a >5% non-linear boost and >5% headroom to a perfect classifier. — [Papadakis et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.01231)
- On WDC Products the symbolic baselines are far behind: Magellan 30.55-41.57 F1 across all 27 variants vs RoBERTa 65.45-87.80; "even with the smallest development sets, the deep learning-based algorithms significantly outperform the symbolic baselines", including for unseen products. — [WDC Products paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.09521)
- Parameter-free ZeroER is "competitive on the DBAC and FOZA datasets, which are relatively well-structured" but underperforms on free-text datasets; authors suggest "potential for developing hybrid methods that combine efficient, parameter-free matchers with other techniques". — [EDBT 2025 cross-dataset study](https://openproceedings.org/2025/conf/edbt/paper-224.pdf)
- Hybrid evidence (industrial, arXiv only, Sept 2025): DistilBERT/all-mpnet-base-v2 bi-encoder alone F1 0.9320 (P 0.9166, R 0.9017) vs transformer + weighted Levenshtein re-check 0.9780 (P 0.9682, R 0.9722) on a web-hosting customer database (email, username, domain fields). The fuzzy layer fixed "typographical mismatches that semantic models may miss". — [Sharifi & Ahmadzadeh, "Transformer-Gather, Fuzzy-Reconsider", arXiv 2509.17470](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.17470v1)
- How BERT matches: in fine-tuned BERT EM models "pair-wise semantic similarity of tokens is not particularly exploited"; attention aligns attributes that describe the same property across the two records. — [Paganelli et al., "Analyzing How BERT Performs Entity Matching", PVLDB 15, 2022](https://www.vldb.org/pvldb/vol15/p1726-paganelli.pdf)
- ByT5 (Xue et al., TACL 2022): byte-level models "are significantly more robust to noise and perform better on tasks that are sensitive to spelling and pronunciation", at the cost of more compute per input (byte sequences are longer). — [ByT5, arXiv 2105.13626](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.13626). ByT5-small and CANINE-s are Apache-2.0. — [google/byt5-small](https://huggingface.co/google/byt5-small), [google/canine-s](https://huggingface.co/google/canine-s)

### Inferences
- Our LightGBM with ~70 features is a "Magellan++" (GBDT instead of RF, more and better-targeted features). The literature suggests its weak spots, relative to a cross-encoder, would be semantics that string similarity cannot express: DBA aliases, pseudo-word replacement names, abbreviation expansion beyond hand-written rules, and long or dirty text. Its strong spots are exact numeric and house-number comparisons, which PLMs handle badly (see Q4). This points to complementary errors, which is the precondition for stacking to help.
- The low-risk design is to add a fine-tuned cross-encoder's out-of-fold probability (and perhaps its logit) as one or more extra LightGBM features, trained with the same group-aware CV folds. Hand-crafted numeric/house-number features then keep veto power over the PLM. This is an inference; no peer-reviewed benchmark measuring such stacking was found.
- With F0.5 already at 0.978, the achievable headroom is small, and benchmark gains quoted for PLMs (+5-30 F1) are measured against much weaker feature baselines (Magellan's generic features), not a tuned 70-feature GBDT. Expect gains closer to the Papadakis "easy dataset" regime (<1-2 points) unless the remaining errors are concentrated in alias/semantic cases.
- For transliterated Hindi/Telugu names, a byte/character-level encoder (ByT5, CANINE) or a multilingual subword model (XLM-R, MIT) is the principled choice, but this needs an ablation on our own data; no EM-specific evidence exists.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study was found that stacks a PLM/LLM match score into a GBDT over similarity features on the standard benchmarks and reports the delta; the TGFR preprint uses a different architecture (bi-encoder + weighted Levenshtein) on private data.
- No EM benchmark result was found for ByT5 or CANINE as matchers; the claims are from general NLP noise-robustness and transliteration tasks.
- No published LightGBM/XGBoost (vs Random Forest) Magellan-style numbers were found on these benchmarks in the sources read.

## Q4. Where PLM/LLM matchers fail (numbers, addresses, near-duplicate distractors)

### Takeaway
The documented failure modes are the ones that matter for business records: look-alike non-matches ("corner cases") hurt precision far more than recall, PLMs/LLMs over-weight overall textual similarity and ignore small but decisive differences (model numbers, prices, years), and all learned matchers lose 10-25+ F1 points on entities not seen in training. Structured numeric comparison is a strength of hand-crafted features, not of LMs.

### Cited Findings
- GPT-4 error analysis on Walmart-Amazon: of 26 false positives, 23 were "overemphasis on matching attributes", 21 "ignoring minor but significant differences", 14 "price discrepancy overlooked"; of 15 false negatives, 9 "model number mismatch", 9 missing/incomplete attributes. On DBLP-Scholar, 26 false positives: 15 over-emphasis on title similarity, 16 author-name similarity overreach, 19 partial-information matching (categories overlap). — [Peeters et al., EDBT 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.11244v4)
- WDC Products: more corner cases reduce precision more than recall for all neural matchers ("mistaking very similar negatives as positives"); e.g. Ditto precision at small dev set / 80% corner cases is 46.28 vs recall 79.33 (seen). Unseen entities "negatively impact the precision of all matchers significantly while recall generally increases". — [WDC Products paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.09521)
- Out-of-distribution collapse: RoBERTa applied to a benchmark it was not trained on loses 22-61% F1. Ditto in the leave-one-dataset-out setting averages 72.9 F1 vs ~87-95 in-domain. — [Peeters et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.11244v4); [EDBT 2025 cross-dataset study](https://openproceedings.org/2025/conf/edbt/paper-224.pdf)
- Long text: Ditto without summarisation scored 41.00 F1 on Company (3,123-word average records, 256-token limit). — [Ditto](https://www.vldb.org/pvldb/vol14/p50-li.pdf)
- Larger fine-tuned models "rely more heavily on shortcut learning". — [Beyond Scale and Generation, arXiv 2607.24688](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24688)
- Small models on domain jargon: AnyMatch-GPT-2 misclassified "sumdex slr camera sling pack" vs "slr camera sling pack" (same brand), which GPT-4o-mini matched correctly; small fine-tuned models trail LLMs by >10 F1 on WDC and Walmart-Amazon. — [EDBT 2025 cross-dataset study](https://openproceedings.org/2025/conf/edbt/paper-224.pdf)
- Fine-tuned 7-8B LLMs remain weak on hard product sets: Llama-3.1-8B fine-tuned Amazon-Google 50.00, Walmart-Amazon 65.65. — [Steiner et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.08185v1)
- In-context demonstrations can hurt: GPT-3.5-Turbo mean F1 falls from 66.0 (none) to 58.8 with hand-picked demonstrations in the cross-dataset setting; GPT-4o-mini 83.9 -> 82.6. — [EDBT 2025 cross-dataset study](https://openproceedings.org/2025/conf/edbt/paper-224.pdf)
- A US patent on "numeric embeddings for entity-matching" describes NLP models wrongly treating numbers that differ by orders of magnitude as matches because their digit patterns look similar (patent text, not a peer-reviewed measurement). — [USPTO 11615120](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11615120)

### Inferences
- House-number noise and branch-level near-duplicates (same chain, different street number) are exactly the "corner case" / "minor but significant difference" pattern where PLMs and LLMs lose precision. Because F0.5 weights precision, a PLM score should not override exact numeric/house-number features. Stacking inside the GBDT, rather than averaging probabilities, lets the GBDT learn when to ignore it.
- Empty addresses reduce the record to name + country. The Ditto "dirty" results suggest PLMs degrade gracefully on missing or misplaced attributes (Dirty vs Structured Walmart-Amazon 85.69 vs 86.76), which is one of the few places the literature predicts a real edge over string features.
- The France-in-test-only situation is the "unseen" setting, where every learned matcher (feature-based or PLM) loses precision. The literature gives no reason to expect a PLM to fix this by itself; generative LLMs are the one family whose advantage "concentrates under distribution shift".

### Gaps
- No study was found that isolates address or house-number errors for PLM/LLM matchers. The evidence is from product model numbers, prices and bibliographic venues, and address behaviour is extrapolated.
- No quantitative evidence was found on PLM/LLM handling of transliterated Indic names or legal-suffix swaps (Pvt Ltd / Private Limited / LLC).
